## Directory intake

The [upstream directory at 4bf796d](https://github.com/n4ze3m/tiny-voca/blob/4bf796d29ea775edd5a7cc5ee3a6806b3c3b91f6/README.md#community) lists 42 language-and-voice entries. Compared with the original 34-entry campaign, eight entries were added and Bengali Voice expanded into Browser Voice. The [dashboard at acaa9e8](https://github.com/ngelik/tts-bench/tree/acaa9e82efdf8bd937050a838ba9e63652e1b2ec/results) has full-article measurements for 33 of the original entries. Kurdish Behdînî remains a [hosted demo](https://behdinan.com/) with no reference browser result.

The original screenshot has 33 measured models across 70 language and backend combinations, including ten English results from five models. Each measurement has three full-article rounds and a 30-second clip from the third round. The export includes the individual round times, median, audio duration, real-time factor, Chrome memory, fixture, voice and links to the recording, timing receipt and separate memory receipt. [Download the original screenshot results (TSV)](./original-screenshot-results.tsv)

The remaining entry, Kurdish Behdînî Voice, sends the text and voice to a hosted service and receives an audio file. Measuring this route would time the service and network. Browser CPU and WebGPU measurement requires a local inference engine for the same 21-voice model. [Publisher’s speech client](https://behdinan.com/js/studio.js?v=693b378474)

Seven spoken languages in the expanded directory lacked measurements at intake, including Uzbek, Mandarin Chinese, Gujarati, Telugu, Sinhala, Persian and Luganda. At intake the only published Portuguese results were for Brazilian Portuguese. Hebrew, Hindi and Malayalam already had boards at intake. Several newer model and script variants still needed separate measurements.

All eight new article editions are live. European Portuguese and Luganda also have published browser adapters. Both passed three-span qualification on one-thread WASM and hardware WebGPU. This was the source-qualification stage. The table above tracks subsequent full-article results for each edition and backend.
